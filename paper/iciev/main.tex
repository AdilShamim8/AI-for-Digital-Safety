\title{\LARGE LightBanglaHC Audit: Evaluation Validity, Inference Reconstruction, and Accounting Boundaries in Harmful-Content Classification}

% Anonymous review: all three author details are entered in CMT.

\begin{abstract}
We audit a stacked TF-IDF ensemble for harmful-content classification, using 47,817 labeled training rows and 11,955 test rows. The recorded weighted ensemble has pooled out-of-fold macro-F1 0.5314, compared with 0.5221 for its strongest base candidate. In a new paired logistic regression run, fitting TF-IDF on all training text yields 0.522061, versus 0.518872 with fold-fitted representations at the source iteration cap. LBFGS reduces this difference to 0.001750. The source-budget difference is about four times the recorded gain from outcome-derived ensemble weights. We also compare nonnested and nested meta training in a reduced three-base stack, obtaining \NonNested{} and \Nested{} respectively with identical outer assessment features. Raw-string checks find 308 duplicate groups spanning folds and 52 groups with conflicting labels. A tiered taxonomy distinguishes label-free preprocessing, aggregate class statistics, supervised feature dependencies, stopping and selection. The profiled identity module is absent from the prediction path. Six base logistic regression fits imply 4,141,908 coefficients and intercepts; the exported value 0.183 is target-derived. Historical outputs, new executions and arithmetic are distinguished. Saved predictions and protocols support replication of this audit, rather than a claim of a new classifier or deployment advantage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A harmful content classifier's reported accuracy and efficiency are interpretable only when they refer to a specified prediction procedure. In sparse ensembles, predictions can depend on fitted representations, calibrated base estimators, and a second-stage head. Counting the head alone does not describe the system that produces its inputs~\cite{schwartz2020greenai}.

We distinguish three objects: the \emph{inference object}, which maps raw text to predictions; the \emph{evaluation object}, which includes fitting, validation, and selection; and the \emph{accounting object}, which defines the artifacts and computation included in a resource claim. Stacking~\cite{wolpert1992stacked} makes their relationship consequential: excluding a row from its own base fit does not necessarily isolate the full procedure when meta training features depend on meta validation labels. Global preprocessing and repeated selection create additional dependencies~\cite{varma2006bias,cawley2010overfitting,kapoor2023leakage}.

We examine one recorded implementation from \CompetitionName. The supplied competition description is an operational source~\cite{herwill2026materials}. The updated notebook, retained outputs, competition description, and raw CSVs permit source inspection, data checks, inference reconstruction, and arithmetic accounting. Historical row-level OOF predictions and fitted artifacts remain unavailable. We execute new data checks, LR sensitivities and a reduced stacking comparison; these are distinct from replaying the original predictions. Documentation describes a mixed Bangla, English, and Banglish corpus; preprocessing alone would not establish this scope.

Our contributions are (1) a source-level reconstruction of the selected prediction and accounting paths, (2) full-data preprocessing and reduced-stacking comparisons with saved row-level probabilities, and (3) duplicate diagnostics and a tiered evaluation-exposure taxonomy. Section~\ref{sec:design} defines the evidence categories. We introduce no new classifier.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Hate speech, offensive language and implicit harm require documented annotation decisions~\cite{davidson2017automated}. Functional tests probe negation and counter speech~\cite{rottger2021hatecheck}; harm and bias analysis requires stating whose interests are considered~\cite{blodgett2020language}. Prior Bengali hate-speech data~\cite{romim2020bengali} and BD-SHS~\cite{romim2022bdshs} and BanglaBERT~\cite{bhattacharjee2022banglabert} provide Bangla context, not directly comparable scores or verified source datasets for this corpus. Data statements help document population and curation scope~\cite{bender2018datastatements}.

Stacked generalization learns from base predictions~\cite{wolpert1992stacked}. Breiman~\cite{breiman1996stacked} studies cross-validated combinations of regression predictions; Ting and Witten~\cite{ting1999issues} examine confidence features and second-level learners for classification. Super Learner studies cross-validated combination and risk minimization~\cite{vanderlaan2007super}; the source's squared-F1 heuristic is different. Hastie et al., Section 7.10.2~\cite{hastie2009elements}, explain why model assessment must include preparation and selection within permitted training data. Their supervised-selection example is not a numerical estimate of label-free TF-IDF bias here. Selection bias~\cite{varma2006bias,cawley2010overfitting} and leakage~\cite{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage} motivate tracing full feature ancestry. Calibration adds fitted state~\cite{niculescumizil2005probabilities} and requires separate reliability diagnostics~\cite{vaicenavicius2019calibration}. Overlapping folds complicate uncertainty~\cite{bengio2004variance}; paired tests require retained predictions and an appropriate design~\cite{dror2018significance}.

Green AI~\cite{schwartz2020greenai}, energy reporting~\cite{henderson2020systematic}, reproducibility~\cite{pineau2021reproducibility}, and model cards~\cite{mitchell2019modelcards} distinguish computation, replay and intended use. This study applies those established principles to one prediction and accounting record.

\section{Audited Prediction Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

\subsection{Cleaning, Signals, and Numerical Features}
Let $x$ be the original string after missing-value handling. The combined processed text is
\begin{equation}
s(x)=C(x)\,\|\,\texttt{SSEP}\,\|\,H(x),
\end{equation}
where $C$ cleans text and $H$ produces signal tokens from the original input. The cleaner applies NFKC normalization, lowercasing, URL and mention substitutions, hashtag-marker removal, emoji substitution, repeated-letter reduction, repeated-punctuation markers, and whitespace compression (S). Signals retain access to original capitalization and punctuation rather than only the cleaned string.

The lexical union has 21 Bangla, 23 transliterated and 29 English entries (73 distinct strings); the subtle inventory has 25 (S). Substring detection counts each distinct entry at most once. Signals repeat capped markers for lexical, sarcasm, threat, identity, script, capitalization, punctuation, length and neutral cues. The string ``this is a salad'' activates \texttt{sala}, a transliterated term used abusively in Bangla and Hindi; it also has benign and kinship uses. Here it is a substring inside ``salad'', not an abusive utterance. The match records a lexical condition rather than intent.

Twenty unscaled numerical features cover length, lexical diversity, punctuation, emoji, script, capitalization, lexical hits and pattern flags. They enter LightGBM only. Bangla regex runs divided by character length are not a fraction of Bangla characters. Cue names are code identifiers, not validated social constructs.

\subsection{Four Globally Fitted Sparse Branches}
Table~\ref{tab:features} lists the feature branches and recorded widths. All use sublinear term frequency and are fitted on all processed training texts before the three-fold split. Test texts are transformed, not fitted on (S). Under the documented default interpretation of omitted smoothed-IDF and normalization options, a branch computes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u_{ij}&=(1+\ln t_{ij})\left[1+\ln\frac{n+1}{\mathrm{df}_j+1}\right],\quad t_{ij}>0,\\
\phi(x_i)&=u_i/\|u_i\|_2,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $u_{ij}=0$ otherwise. Historical package versions and fitted arrays are absent; current documentation is not evidence of the original runtime.\footnote{Consulted API: \url{https://scikit-learn.org/1.5/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_extraction.text.TfidfVectorizer.html}.}

\subsection{OOF Features and Selected Test Predictor}
Let $V_1,V_2,V_3$ partition $I=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and let $f(i)$ identify row $i$'s fold. For base candidate $b$, let $h_b^{(-f)}$ be its probability-producing fit excluding $V_f$, with calibration or early stopping as specified. Training and test meta features differ:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
z_i&=\left[h_b^{(-f(i))}(\phi(x_i))\right]_{b=1}^{6}\in\mathbb{R}^{18},\\
\bar z(x)&=\left[\frac{1}{3}\sum_{f=1}^{3}h_b^{(-f)}(\phi(x))\right]_{b=1}^{6}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
LightGBM additionally receives the numerical features, suppressed in this notation for readability. The notation excludes a fold from its base estimator fit, not from global preprocessing, class weights, or LightGBM stopping selection.

Three Meta-LR fits are trained from the single global OOF feature matrix using the same split scheme. Their test probabilities are averaged. The selected final predictor combines six averaged base outputs and the averaged Meta-LR output, so Meta-LR is an active component rather than merely an alternative candidate. If $F_j$ is candidate $j$'s pooled OOF macro-F1 and $p_j(x)$ its test probability vector, then
\begin{equation}
\alpha_j=\frac{F_j^2}{\sum_{k=1}^{7}F_k^2},\qquad
\hat y(x)=\arg\max_c\sum_{j=1}^{7}\alpha_j p_j(x)_c.
\label{eq:ensemble}
\end{equation}
These weights use labeled development outcomes rather than a prespecified average. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} separates this prediction path from the independent profiling operation.

\label{sec:validity}

\subsection{Different Types of Evaluation Exposure}

The TF-IDF fits precede fold construction, but test text is only transformed. Thus this is intra-training covariate exposure, not fitting on the unlabeled test set. This small statistic difference should not be presented as equivalent in scope to the meta learner's full supervised ancestry. It does not by itself prove a negligible prediction effect.

\subsection{Nonnested Meta-Training Dependencies}
For a meta learner $q^{(-a)}$ trained on $\{(z_i,y_i):i\notin V_a\}$ and evaluated on $V_a$, take a meta-training row $i\in V_b$, $b\ne a$. Its features came from base fits on $I\setminus V_b$, a set containing $V_a$. Thus
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_{V_a}&\longrightarrow h_m^{(-b)}\longrightarrow z_i\\
&\longrightarrow\text{meta-training data}\longrightarrow q^{(-a)}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:path}
\end{equation}
A row in $V_a$ does receive base features from an estimator fit excluding $V_a$. However, the meta model evaluating that row was trained on other features whose construction used $y_{V_a}$. Row-level OOF exclusion therefore does not isolate the complete stacker. This structural dependency remains even if global preprocessing and class weights are provisionally accepted.

Rebuilding base OOF features only once for all meta folds preserves this path. It must instead be regenerated within the training partition of each outer assessment split, and again within the allowed fitting subset of each selection split. Equation~\ref{eq:path} establishes missing isolation, not the magnitude or direction of score distortion. The reduced comparison below executes both meta-training constructions with the same outer assessment probabilities.

\subsection{Stopping, Near-Uniform Weights, and Selection}
LightGBM uses its assessment labels for stopping and later reports macro-F1 on the same rows. Reporting a different objective does not create a new assessment sample. Ensemble weights are functions of the pooled OOF F1 values, so each evaluation label can also affect the aggregation rule.

Using the seven printed four-decimal candidate scores in Equation~\ref{eq:ensemble} gives approximate weights 0.154991, 0.158125, 0.134992, 0.111846, 0.127651, 0.154452, and 0.157943 for LR\_C8, LR\_C3, SVC, CNB, SGD, LightGBM, and Meta-LR respectively (A). Uniform weight is $1/7=0.142857$; the largest absolute departure is about 0.031011. The source used unrounded scores, so these are reconstructed approximations, not recovered fitted weights.

The recorded weighted score 0.5314 exceeds the simple average 0.5306 by 0.0008, or 0.08 percentage points (A). This is a small observed development-set gain. It is \emph{not} an upper bound on leakage or selection optimism: both probability arrays share other evaluation dependencies, and no untouched paired comparison is available. We therefore give this mechanism narrower emphasis than meta-feature ancestry.

Each threshold grid assesses 900 choices on the same OOF labels. Their selected results and unadjusted alternatives are then compared to choose one of four final variants. These are selection opportunities~\cite{varma2006bias,cawley2010overfitting}; no measured bias is assigned to them or to the rounded 0.0093 ensemble-versus-base difference.

\subsection{Dependence and Uncertainty}
The three fitting subsets overlap: each pair shares roughly one-third of the full corpus, or half of either fitting subset. They are not independent repetitions. Stratification addresses class balance but does not protect against duplicates, shared authors, threads, or temporal dependence. Seed-42 fold indices are now regenerated from the supplied CSV using scikit-learn 1.8.0. They are saved with IDs and labels. They are a new reconstruction, not an authenticated historical fold file; their duplicate counts appear below.

There is no universally unbiased K-fold variance estimator based only on observed fold errors~\cite{bengio2004variance}. Paired comparisons also require retained row level predictions and a procedure appropriate to the data's independent units~\cite{dror2018significance}. We bootstrap paired fixed OOF predictions below. This conditions on the fitted models, omits training uncertainty, and supports no claim of general predictive superiority.

\section{Parameter Boundaries and Score Provenance}
\label{sec:accounting}

\subsection{The Identity Module Is a Different Object}
The source defines \texttt{ZeroParamNet.forward(x)} to return $x$ unchanged. It has no trainable parameters. Profiling, when available, calls this module on a random three-element vector after the selected predictions already exist (S). No invocation applies the module to raw text, base probabilities, meta features, or selected outputs. Its zero count therefore describes a separate identity operation, not the harmful-content predictor.

The retained output prints \texttt{Manual NOP: 0} and \texttt{Final thop NOP: 0}, without the success line printed by the successful profiling branch. The variable is initialized to zero and remains zero when THOP is unavailable or fails. Consequently, the final printed zero alone cannot establish successful profiling. The source uses one positional input to this separate module, whereas the supplied competition description illustrates a two-input model call with token IDs and an attention mask. These interfaces do not determine a whole-system accounting boundary.

\subsection{Structural Inventory of the Selected Predictor}
The selected path needs six three-fold base candidates, three Meta-LR fits, seven weights, four fitted vocabularies/IDF arrays and fixed text/numerical rules. No learned PyTorch head is invoked.

At recorded TF-IDF width $D=230{,}105$, each three-class multinomial LR fit has $3D$ coefficients and three intercepts. The two LR candidates each require three fits for their stored test averages. Meta-LR has 18 input probabilities and three output scores:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{one\ base\ LR}}&=3D+3=690{,}318,\\
N_{\mathrm{six\ base\ LR}}&=6(3D+3)=4{,}141{,}908,\\
N_{\mathrm{three\ Meta\ LR}}&=3(3\times18+3)=171.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:counts}
\end{equation}
These are arithmetic scalar inventories for specified matrices (A), not measured memory occupancy or a complete contest NOP. Coefficients for SVC and SGD, CNB state, calibrators, LightGBM trees, vocabularies, and IDF arrays remain outside this subtotal. Internal calibration can retain multiple fitted classifiers/functions per outer object; historical defaults and fitted internals are unknown~\cite{pedregosa2011scikit}.

No complete serialized artifact, peak RAM measurement, raw-text inference latency, or energy benchmark is available. Scalar counts, fitted state, bytes, RAM, latency and energy are distinct~\cite{henderson2020systematic}. Comparisons need the same boundary and models that generate probabilities for unseen text.

\subsection{Fractional Export Values Are Not Measurements}
The source sets the main export field by rounding $F/2.9$ to three decimal places (S), producing 0.183 (R/D).
This is a target-derived denominator, not a structural parameter measurement. The assigned values 0.183, 0.1, and 0.3 are fractional, whereas a count of scalar parameters is an integer. The separate identity operation has zero trainable parameters; the selected fitted ensemble has additional state. These observations describe implementation and measurement provenance, not a verified organizer ruling or a claim about author intent.

With $F=0.5314177984731835$ and $\epsilon=5\times10^{-16}$, the local ratio $F/(N+\epsilon)$ gives about $1.0628\times10^{15}$ at $N=0$ and 2.9039 at $N=0.183$ (A). These are arithmetic projections, not verified leaderboard scores.

\subsection{Authoritative Scoring Remains Unresolved}
The supplied details document gives $0.4F+0.6/[\log(N+1)+\epsilon]$ without a log base, but later quotes $F/(N+\epsilon)$. The original formula image, official scoring code and classical-model count boundary are not retained. The notebook's local ratio is not verification of the contest contract. For the logarithmic expression, assuming natural logarithms and $N=0.183$ gives an efficiency term approximately 3.57029 (A); a small fractional denominator can therefore dominate that hypothetical score. Neither numerical plausibility nor a reconstructed score establishes the organizer's rule. We report no hidden-set F1 or leaderboard result. Supplement S1 preserves version provenance without treating unverified platform reports as measurements.

\section{Corrected Prospective Protocol}
\label{sec:discussion}
Freeze data bytes, label meanings, sampling units and permitted uses. Inspect duplicate/conflict groups and document label-conflict treatment. Keep row-stratified and group-aware assessments separate. Within each outer training subset, rebuild inner base OOF features, fit preprocessing and class weights locally, and keep calibration and stopping inside training data. Prespecify the assessment predictor: our reduced stack uses one outer-training base fit in both arms; a fold-averaged predictor requires a matched evaluation.

For the complete weighted ensemble, reserve combination weights, thresholds and candidate choice for training-only selection. The reduced stack does not validate these additional steps. Preserve IDs, folds, probabilities, versions, optimizer diagnostics and fitted objects. Reload the complete raw-text predictor and check probability equivalence before measuring bytes, peak RAM, latency or energy~\cite{pineau2021reproducibility}. Define contest NOP from the original rules. Supplement S2 identifies executable scripts and retained outputs.

\section{Limitations and Responsible Interpretation}
\label{sec:limitations}
The updated source and retained outputs are inspectable, and the supplied CSVs agree with its printed supports. However, no hash recorded during the historical run establishes that those exact bytes were used. The audit regenerates folds, checks a new export, and executes LR and reduced-stacking comparisons. It does not recover historical OOF rows, inspect historical fitted states, or verify platform acceptance. Unknown package versions and contradictory device metadata limit replay. Exact-string duplication, conflicting labels and regenerated cross-fold allocation are checked. Semantic duplicates and shared-user/thread structure remain unexamined.

The threats concern fitting and selection exposure, one-record generalization, incomplete annotation documentation, dependent observations and mismatched accounting boundaries. Controlled differences quantify specific protocol changes, not total historical optimism or deployment cost. Three-way argmax is not a moderation policy: substring and identity cues do not establish endorsement, quotation or reclaimed usage~\cite{blodgett2020language,rottger2021hatecheck}. No deployment safety, appeal process or resource advantage is measured. Public-source collection does not itself establish consent or redistribution permission.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This case study reconstructs the updated ensemble record: 47,817 training rows, four fitted TF-IDF branches, six base candidates, a Meta-LR component, and a selected weighted ensemble. Its reconstructed macro-F1 0.531418 agrees with the notebook's 0.5314; the rounded comparison with LR\_C3 at 0.5221 remains descriptive. Regenerated folds reveal repeated and conflicting text groups; the new CSV matches recorded export structure and class totals. Historical versions are documented in Supplement S1. Global TF-IDF raises macro-F1 by 0.003189 at the source SAGA budget; the converged LBFGS sensitivity gives a smaller difference, 0.001750. The reduced stack comparison gives \NonNested{} for nonnested and \Nested{} for nested meta training, with the same outer assessment features. Both results make the evaluation protocol part of the reported outcome; neither measures the bias of every historical selection step.

Row-level OOF exclusion does not isolate the full procedure when assessment labels remain ancestors of meta-training features or selection. A zero-parameter identity operation omits the fitted predictor that supplies its inputs, and a target-derived fractional denominator is not a parameter measurement. Predictive and efficiency claims need a specified inference object, fitting ancestry, accounting boundary and evidence type.
