\begin{abstract}
We audit a stacked TF-IDF ensemble for harmful-content classification and trace how evaluation and parameter accounting relate to the predictor actually used. The recorded three-fold run contains 47,817 labeled training rows, 11,955 test rows, 230,105 TF-IDF features, six base candidates, and a logistic regression stacker. The selected weighted ensemble records pooled out-of-fold macro-F1 0.5314, compared with 0.5221 for the strongest base candidate. We distinguish label-free representation fitting, aggregate class statistics, supervised meta-feature dependencies, stopping, and selection. Regenerated seed-42 folds place 308 exact-text groups across folds; 52 repeated-text groups contain conflicting labels. Reconstructed ensemble weights range approximately from 0.111846 to 0.158125. Their observed advantage over uniform averaging is 0.0008, not a bound on selection bias. The separately profiled identity module is absent from the prediction path. Six base logistic regression fits imply 4,141,908 coefficients and intercepts, while the exported value 0.183 is derived from a target ratio. Source observations, retained outputs, data checks, and arithmetic are labeled separately. A paired LR comparison yields macro-F1 0.522061 with global TF-IDF and 0.518872 with fold-fitted TF-IDF, holding class weights fixed. The audit supplies controlled rerun protocols. It introduces no new classifier or measured deployment advantage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A harmful content classifier's reported accuracy and efficiency are interpretable only when they refer to a specified prediction procedure. In sparse ensembles, predictions can depend on fitted representations, calibrated base estimators, and a second-stage head. Counting the head alone does not describe the system that produces its inputs~\cite{schwartz2020greenai}.

We distinguish three objects: the \emph{inference object}, which maps raw text to predictions; the \emph{evaluation object}, which includes fitting, validation, and selection; and the \emph{accounting object}, which defines the artifacts and computation included in a resource claim. Stacking~\cite{wolpert1992stacked} makes their relationship consequential: excluding a row from its own base fit does not necessarily isolate the full procedure when meta training features depend on meta validation labels. Global preprocessing and repeated selection create additional dependencies~\cite{varma2006bias,cawley2010overfitting,kapoor2023leakage}.

We examine one recorded implementation from \CompetitionName. \ifNamedCase The supplied competition description is cited as an operational source~\cite{herwill2026materials}.\fi The updated notebook, retained outputs, competition description, and raw CSVs permit source inspection, data checks, inference reconstruction, and arithmetic accounting. Historical row-level OOF predictions and fitted artifacts remain unavailable. We execute new data checks and a controlled LR comparison; these are distinct from replaying the original predictions. Documentation describes a mixed Bangla, English, and Banglish corpus; preprocessing alone would not establish this scope.

The contribution is an inspectable audit linking the three objects within one artifact, followed by a corrected prospective protocol. It is not a new algorithm, a demonstration of predictive superiority, or a measured deployment advantage. Section~\ref{sec:design} defines the evidence categories and verification limits used throughout.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Hate speech, offensive language and implicit harm require documented annotation decisions~\cite{davidson2017automated}. Functional tests probe negation and counter speech~\cite{rottger2021hatecheck}; harm and bias analysis requires stating whose interests are considered~\cite{blodgett2020language}. BD-SHS~\cite{romim2022bdshs} and BanglaBERT~\cite{bhattacharjee2022banglabert} provide Bangla context, not directly comparable scores or verified source datasets for this corpus. Data statements help document population and curation scope~\cite{bender2018datastatements}.

Stacked generalization learns from base predictions~\cite{wolpert1992stacked}. Super Learner studies cross-validated combination and risk minimization~\cite{vanderlaan2007super}; the source's squared-F1 heuristic is different. Hastie et al., Section 7.10.2~\cite{hastie2009elements}, explain why model assessment must include preparation and selection within permitted training data. Their supervised-selection example is not a numerical estimate of label-free TF-IDF bias here. Selection bias~\cite{varma2006bias,cawley2010overfitting} and leakage~\cite{kapoor2023leakage} motivate tracing full feature ancestry. Calibration adds fitted state~\cite{niculescumizil2005probabilities} and requires separate reliability diagnostics~\cite{vaicenavicius2019calibration}. Overlapping folds complicate uncertainty~\cite{bengio2004variance}; paired tests require retained predictions and an appropriate design~\cite{dror2018significance}.

Green AI~\cite{schwartz2020greenai}, energy reporting~\cite{henderson2020systematic}, reproducibility~\cite{pineau2021reproducibility}, and model cards~\cite{mitchell2019modelcards} distinguish computation, replay and intended use. This study applies those established principles to one prediction and accounting record.

\section{Audited Prediction Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

\subsection{Cleaning, Signals, and Numerical Features}
Let $x$ be the original string after missing-value handling. The combined processed text is
\begin{equation}
s(x)=C(x)\,\|\,\texttt{SSEP}\,\|\,H(x),
\end{equation}
where $C$ cleans text and $H$ produces signal tokens from the original input. The cleaner applies NFKC normalization, lowercasing, URL and mention substitutions, hashtag-marker removal, emoji substitution, repeated-letter reduction, repeated-punctuation markers, and whitespace compression (S). Signals retain access to original capitalization and punctuation rather than only the cleaned string.

The explicit lexical union contains 21 Bangla, 23 transliterated, and 29 English entries, totaling 73 distinct strings; the subtle inventory contains 25 entries (S). Lexical detection tests substring presence, counting each distinct entry at most once per text, rather than counting every occurrence. Signals include capped repetition of explicit, subtle, sarcasm, threat, identity-related, script, capitalization, punctuation, length, and neutral markers. The string ``this is a salad'' activates \texttt{sala}, a transliterated term used abusively in Bangla and Hindi; it also has benign and kinship uses. Here it is a substring inside ``salad'', not an abusive utterance. The match records a lexical condition rather than intent.

Twenty numerical features are generated without fitted scaling. They cover length, word count, lexical diversity, punctuation, emoji runs, Bangla regex runs, script indicators, capitalization, lexical hits, pattern flags, a combined cue count, and short/long indicators. One feature divides the number of Bangla regex runs by character length; it is not a measured fraction of Bangla characters. Numerical features are supplied to LightGBM only. A marker named for hate or neutrality is a code identifier, not a validated social construct.

\subsection{Four Globally Fitted Sparse Branches}
Table~\ref{tab:features} lists the feature branches and recorded widths. All use sublinear term frequency and are fitted on all processed training texts before the three-fold split. Test texts are transformed, not fitted on (S). Under the documented default interpretation of omitted smoothed-IDF and normalization options, a branch computes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u_{ij}&=(1+\ln t_{ij})\left[1+\ln\frac{n+1}{\mathrm{df}_j+1}\right],\quad t_{ij}>0,\\
\phi(x_i)&=u_i/\|u_i\|_2,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $u_{ij}=0$ otherwise. Historical package versions and fitted arrays are absent; current documentation is not evidence of the original runtime.\footnote{Consulted API: \url{https://scikit-learn.org/1.5/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_extraction.text.TfidfVectorizer.html}.}

\subsection{OOF Features and Selected Test Predictor}
Let $V_1,V_2,V_3$ partition $I=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and let $f(i)$ identify row $i$'s fold. For base candidate $b$, let $h_b^{(-f)}$ be its probability-producing fit excluding $V_f$, with calibration or early stopping as specified. Training and test meta features differ:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
z_i&=\left[h_b^{(-f(i))}(\phi(x_i))\right]_{b=1}^{6}\in\mathbb{R}^{18},\\
\bar z(x)&=\left[\frac{1}{3}\sum_{f=1}^{3}h_b^{(-f)}(\phi(x))\right]_{b=1}^{6}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
LightGBM additionally receives the numerical features, suppressed in this notation for readability. The notation excludes a fold from its base estimator fit, not from global preprocessing, class weights, or LightGBM stopping selection.

Three Meta-LR fits are trained from the single global OOF feature matrix using the same split scheme. Their test probabilities are averaged. The selected final predictor combines six averaged base outputs and the averaged Meta-LR output, so Meta-LR is an active component rather than merely an alternative candidate. If $F_j$ is candidate $j$'s pooled OOF macro-F1 and $p_j(x)$ its test probability vector, then
\begin{equation}
\alpha_j=\frac{F_j^2}{\sum_{k=1}^{7}F_k^2},\qquad
\hat y(x)=\arg\max_c\sum_{j=1}^{7}\alpha_j p_j(x)_c.
\label{eq:ensemble}
\end{equation}
These weights use labeled development outcomes rather than a prespecified average. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} separates this prediction path from the independent profiling operation.

\section{Evaluation Validity}
\label{sec:validity}

\subsection{Different Types of Evaluation Exposure}
The retained score describes development predictions. Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} separates mechanisms by the information they use and their structural reach. The tiers prioritize validation work; they are not measured effect sizes or a universal ordering of harm.

The TF-IDF fits precede fold construction, but test text is only transformed. Thus this is intra-training covariate exposure, not fitting on the unlabeled test set. Global class weights use $w_{I,c}=|I|/(3n_{I,c})$. Regenerated fold-local weights differ from these global values by at most 0.005506\% relatively (A), reflecting the close stratified class counts. This small statistic difference should not be presented as equivalent in scope to the meta learner's full supervised ancestry. It does not by itself prove a negligible prediction effect.

\subsection{Nonnested Meta-Training Dependencies}
For a meta learner $q^{(-a)}$ trained on $\{(z_i,y_i):i\notin V_a\}$ and evaluated on $V_a$, take a meta-training row $i\in V_b$, $b\ne a$. Its features came from base fits on $I\setminus V_b$, a set containing $V_a$. Thus
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_{V_a}&\longrightarrow h_m^{(-b)}\longrightarrow z_i\\
&\longrightarrow\text{meta-training data}\longrightarrow q^{(-a)}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:path}
\end{equation}
A row in $V_a$ does receive base features from an estimator fit excluding $V_a$. However, the meta model evaluating that row was trained on other features whose construction used $y_{V_a}$. Row-level OOF exclusion therefore does not isolate the complete stacker. This structural dependency remains even if global preprocessing and class weights are provisionally accepted.

Rebuilding base OOF features only once for all meta folds preserves this path. It must instead be regenerated within the training partition of each outer assessment split, and again within the allowed fitting subset of each selection split. Equation~\ref{eq:path} establishes missing isolation, not the magnitude or direction of score distortion. The nested comparison in the supplement is prospective; its result remains an explicit author task.

\subsection{Stopping, Near-Uniform Weights, and Selection}
LightGBM uses its assessment labels for stopping and later reports macro-F1 on the same rows. Reporting a different objective does not create a new assessment sample. Ensemble weights are functions of the pooled OOF F1 values, so each evaluation label can also affect the aggregation rule.

Using the seven printed four-decimal candidate scores in Equation~\ref{eq:ensemble} gives approximate weights 0.154991, 0.158125, 0.134992, 0.111846, 0.127651, 0.154452, and 0.157943 for LR\_C8, LR\_C3, SVC, CNB, SGD, LightGBM, and Meta-LR respectively (A). Uniform weight is $1/7=0.142857$; the largest absolute departure is about 0.031011. The source used unrounded scores, so these are reconstructed approximations, not recovered fitted weights.

The recorded weighted score 0.5314 exceeds the simple average 0.5306 by 0.0008, or 0.08 percentage points (A). This is a small observed development-set gain. It is \emph{not} an upper bound on leakage or selection optimism: both probability arrays share other evaluation dependencies, and no untouched paired comparison is available. We therefore give this mechanism narrower emphasis than meta-feature ancestry.

Each threshold grid assesses 900 choices on the same OOF labels. Their selected results and unadjusted alternatives are then compared to choose one of four final variants. These are selection opportunities~\cite{varma2006bias,cawley2010overfitting}; no measured bias is assigned to them or to the rounded 0.0093 ensemble-versus-base difference.

\subsection{Dependence and Uncertainty}
The three fitting subsets overlap: each pair shares roughly one-third of the full corpus, or half of either fitting subset. They are not independent repetitions. Stratification addresses class balance but does not protect against duplicates, shared authors, threads, or temporal dependence. Seed-42 fold indices are now regenerated from the supplied CSV using scikit-learn 1.8.0. They are saved with IDs and labels. They are a new reconstruction, not an authenticated historical fold file; their duplicate counts appear below.

There is no universally unbiased K-fold variance estimator based only on observed fold errors~\cite{bengio2004variance}. Paired comparisons also require retained row level predictions and a procedure appropriate to the data's independent units~\cite{dror2018significance}. We report neither a naive fold-based interval nor a significance claim. Such an interval would not resolve the fitting and selection exposures already traced.

\section{Corrected Prospective Protocol}
\label{sec:discussion}
Freeze data bytes, label meanings, sampling units and permitted uses before assessment. Inspect duplicate/conflict groups; grouping protects repeated text while label conflicts require documented treatment. Keep the regenerated row-stratified comparison separate from a future group-aware evaluation.

For each outer training subset $T_a$ and assessment subset $E_a$, build new inner three-fold base OOF features using only $T_a$. Fit the four TF-IDF branches and class weights within permitted fitting subsets. Calibration remains inside each base fitting subset. LightGBM stopping uses a further training-only partition, never $E_a$. Fit Meta-LR on the new inner OOF features, and generate $E_a$ base features by averaging the same inner fits. This evaluates a specified fold-averaged base predictor. It does not silently equate it with a different final deployment ensemble.

Run the original non-nested arm in the same new environment and save its paired probabilities. The fully corrected nested arm changes preprocessing, class statistics, stopping and feature ancestry jointly; its score difference is a protocol comparison, not a causal estimate of stacking alone. A separate nesting-only sensitivity study must hold the other operations fixed. Reserve ensemble weights, threshold search and candidate choice for training-only selection if evaluating the full selected ensemble. The requested Meta-LR comparison does not automatically validate that full ensemble.

Preserve IDs, labels, folds, probabilities, versions, convergence warnings, fitted objects and stopping choices. Serialize/reload the complete raw-text predictor and check probability equivalence before measuring bytes, peak RAM, latency or energy~\cite{pineau2021reproducibility}. Define contest NOP using its original rules. Supplement S2 supplies the runnable LR script and complete stacking protocol; unperformed studies retain explicit author tasks.

\section{Limitations and Responsible Interpretation}
\label{sec:limitations}
The updated source and retained outputs are inspectable, and the supplied CSVs agree with its printed supports. However, no hash recorded during the historical run establishes that those exact bytes were used. The audit regenerates folds, checks a new export, and executes a controlled LR comparison. It does not recover historical OOF rows, inspect historical fitted states, or verify platform acceptance. Unknown package versions and contradictory device metadata limit replay. Exact-string duplication, conflicting labels and regenerated cross-fold allocation are checked. Semantic duplicates and shared-user/thread structure remain unexamined.

The threats are distinct: internal, because fitting and selection use evaluation information; external, because one record does not establish a population claim; construct-related, because label definitions lack a complete annotation manual; statistical, because folds and selection outcomes are dependent; and measurement-related, because the counted identity object differs from the selected predictor. Neither the direction of score distortion nor deployment cost is measured.

The three-way argmax is a research prediction, not a moderation policy. Substring matching can activate inside benign words, and identity-related cues do not model whether a speaker endorses, condemns, quotes, or reclaims an expression~\cite{blodgett2020language,rottger2021hatecheck}. No operating threshold, contextual safety, appeal process, or deployment benefit has been established. No legal basis, consent, ethics approval, or independent redistribution permission is inferred from public-source collection or from a supplied license description.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This case study reconstructs the updated ensemble record: 47,817 training rows, four fitted TF-IDF branches, six base candidates, a Meta-LR component, and a selected weighted ensemble. Its reconstructed macro-F1 0.531418 agrees with the notebook's 0.5314; the rounded comparison with LR\_C3 at 0.5221 remains descriptive. Regenerated folds reveal repeated and conflicting text groups; the new CSV matches recorded export structure and class totals. Historical versions remain supplemental.

Three findings transfer. Row-level OOF exclusion does not isolate a full procedure when evaluation labels remain ancestors of meta-training features, stopping choices, or combination weights. A zero-parameter identity operation does not account for the fitted state that produces harmful-content predictions. A fractional denominator selected to achieve a desired score is not a parameter measurement. Every predictive or efficiency claim should name its inference object, fitting and selection ancestry, accounting boundary, and supporting evidence. This remains an inspectable methodological case, not a validated deployment system.
