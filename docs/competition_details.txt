HerWILL: AI for Digital Safety Datathon 2026
Overview & Description
The primary objective of this competition is to advance the development of Machine Learning and Deep Learning models that can accurately and efficiently classify harmful content on social media in the form of text. Harmful language whether explicit, subtle or indirect can negatively impact individuals, communities and public discourse, particularly in online environments where content spreads rapidly and at scale.
By developing robust harmful content classification systems, this competition aims to contribute to safer digital spaces and encourage responsible AI solutions that support respectful communication and protect vulnerable groups. The HerWILL: AI for Digital Safety Datathon 2026 seeks to foster meaningful engagement across the Data Science, Natural Language Processing, Social Sciences and Digital Safety research communities, while emphasizing both technical excellence and social responsibility.

Dataset Description
Participants will work with a large-scale dataset of approximately 59,772 manually annotated social media posts drawn from publicly accessible Bangla, English, and Banglish platforms. The data consists of comments, captions, headlines, and status updates reflecting real-world linguistic diversity and complexity.
The task is a multi-class text classification problem where participants must classify social media text into three categories:
0:
 Explicitly Toxic/Hateful
1:
 Subtly Toxic (e.g., sarcasm, coded language)
2:
 Non-toxic/Neutral
Evaluation Metric
We use a custom metric called the 
F1NOP Score
, which strongly rewards efficient models, not just accurate ones. Based on the provided formula image, it is defined as:
F1NOP = (0.4 × F1 Score) + (0.6 × (1 / (log(NOP + 1) + ε)))
Where the F1 Score is the macro F1 across 3 classes, NOP is the Number of Parameters, and ε = 5 × 10⁻¹⁶.
Number of Parameters (NOP)
Participants must compute model parameters using the thop library as follows:
from thop import profile
def measure_nop(model, inputs):
    flops, params = profile(model, inputs=(inputs['input_ids'], inputs['attention_mask']))
    return flops, params
The NOP must be reported in the submission file.
